# Visual style guide — locked

**Why this file exists.** Twice now, artwork has been generated "in the style" of the channel from a written description and come out wrong: blank white disc heads with dot eyes, cold grey-blue palettes, generic semi-realistic rendering. That is not the channel's look. The user rejected both passes and told me to go back to the first video's image batches.

This guide is derived from the actual approved artwork in Drive. Read it before writing any image prompt, and **always attach a real reference image** from the list at the bottom. A prompt alone will drift.

---

## The single most important rule

**Every face must act.**

The heads are large, round and white, and they are the entire performance. Shot after shot in the approved batches, the expression carries the joke or the emotion — the dialogue is in the eyebrows. A frame with a blank face and dot eyes is a failed frame, no matter how good the lighting is.

### How to draw an approved face

- **Eyes:** big oval white eyes with visible whites and a distinct dark pupil, set wide. Not dots, not small circles.
- **Eyebrows:** thick, dark, and doing the work — angled down for anger, raised for alarm, flat and heavy for deadpan, one raised for scepticism.
- **Mouths:** wide and varied — open in a shout or a laugh, a smug curve, a flat line, a tiny "o" of worry, an open-mouthed snore.
- **Hair:** a shaggy dark tuft or mop, often slightly chaotic. Gives silhouette and character.
- **Stick limbs:** thin, simple black lines. Hands are minimal — a few fingers at most.
- **Optional extras used sparingly:** under-eye bags for exhaustion, a single sweat drop, blush for embarrassment.

## Line and fill

- Thick, warm, slightly irregular ink outlines — heavier than the Video 03 previous pass, closer to marker than to pen.
- Flat or lightly shaded fills. Simple cel-style shadow, no photographic texture, no grain, no render sheen.
- Backgrounds are *more* detailed than the characters: reeds, stones, grass tufts, hide structures, tree lines.

## Palette — warm, saturated, never clinical

Approved images are rich and colourful even at night.

- **Day / golden hour:** amber, ochre, dusty rose, warm tan ground, pale cyan-green sky.
- **Night:** deep indigo, plum and violet — **not** flat grey or steel blue. Firelight is strong orange-amber and pools visibly on faces and ground.
- **Interiors (modern):** warm muted plum and mauve, not office grey.
- Modern blue phone-screen light is the **only** cold light allowed, and it must be surrounded by warm darkness.

## Characters

- **Renn** — tall, round white face, dark hair tuft, rough brown hide tunic, long wooden spear. Performs confidence he hasn't earned.
- **Sena** — ochre-patterned hide, hair tuft, same big eyes but flat, unimpressed brows. Deadpan.
- **Tep** — small child, same head, huge wide eyes, tiny hide wrap. Always asking something.
- **Modern lead** — round white face, messy hair, plain pale t-shirt, stick limbs.

Consistency matters more than variety: same head shape, same hair, same clothing, same proportions in every shot.

## Composition

- 16:9.
- Broad establishing shots alternating with tight single-subject close-ups — the close-up is where the expression lands.
- Fire, spear, fish, hides, stones, baskets, beds, phones as recurring props.
- Keep modern and prehistoric scenes in the same visual language; only the props change.

## Hard don'ts

- No photoreal animals, no cinematic photography, no stock-footage look.
- No blank white disc heads with dot eyes.
- No grey-blue colour grading.
- No on-screen text, dates, labels or disclaimers baked into the artwork — those are set separately if needed.
- No cave settings unless the script calls for one.
