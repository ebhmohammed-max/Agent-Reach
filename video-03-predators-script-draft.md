# Video 03 — What Was Waiting at the Lake

**Editorial direction (v3).** Story, not lecture. Direct address to the viewer. Named characters. A modern-day hook that lands before any prehistory appears. No on-screen or spoken disclaimers, no "this is imagined", no "scientists think maybe". The narration opens by describing the viewer's own night, then hands that feeling to Renn.

**Characters.** Renn — the main character, tall, brown hide, carries the spear, talks too much. Sena — ochre hide, practical, unimpressed. Tep — the child.

**Running time.** Roughly 6–7 minutes.

---

## Narration script (v3)

### The hook

It's eleven at night. You're walking back to your car. The street is empty, the lights are behind you, and then you hear something.

A sound. Behind you. In the dark, somewhere you can't quite see.

And you don't run. You don't spin around. You just walk a little faster, and you tell yourself it was nothing.

That feeling — that exact one, the one in the back of your neck — is older than farming. Older than cities. Older than every word you've ever spoken.

It's about two hundred thousand years old.

And once, it belonged to a man named Renn.

### Renn's evening

Two hundred thousand years ago, near a lake in what is now northern Germany, the sun is going down and Renn has not stopped talking.

He's tall. He's wearing a hide that used to be a horse. He's carrying a wooden spear that took somebody days to make, and he is explaining — again — how good he is with it.

Sena has heard this before. She's in the ochre hide, she's gutting a fish, and she is not looking up.

Tep, who is eight, is looking up. Tep thinks Renn is the greatest hunter who has ever lived. Tep is wrong, but it's sweet.

This is the part nobody puts in the documentaries. Not the spears, not the bones. This. A family at the water's edge, arguing about dinner, at the end of an ordinary day.

And then the reeds move.

### The sound

Renn stops mid-sentence.

Not because he's brave. Because he's a talker, and a talker knows exactly what silence sounds like when it isn't his.

Sena's hands go still over the fish. Tep looks at the reeds, then at Renn, then at the reeds again.

Nobody says anything. The water laps. Something moves again — a shape, sliding between the trees on the far side, too big for a bird and too quiet for a person.

Sena picks up a stone. Renn picks up his spear, which he is holding by the wrong end, and does not notice.

And then the shape steps out of the trees, and it's a deer.

A young one. Thin, twitchy, ridiculous. It blinks at them like they've interrupted something.

Renn exhales, says something clever, and lowers his spear. Sena shakes her head. Tep laughs — actually laughs, out loud, the way kids do when the adults have been frightened.

And then the deer stops looking at them.

It's looking past them. At the reeds. Behind their backs.

### What came out of the trees

The deer runs.

And what walks out after it is not a lion, though your brain will try to tell you it is. It's heavier through the shoulders. Its front legs are too long. And when it opens its mouth, you see the teeth.

Two of them. Long as your hand. Curving down past its jaw like a pair of blades.

Homotherium. The sabre-toothed cat of Europe. A predator that stood about as tall as a person at the shoulder and weighed more than three of Renn put together.

And it is standing between Renn's family and the path home.

Now — everything you've seen in films says Renn should charge it. Roar at it. Wave the spear around.

Renn does something far less cinematic, and far smarter.

He doesn't move at all.

Sena doesn't move. Tep doesn't move. The three of them stand there like they've been switched off, because that is what living things do when something with teeth is deciding.

The cat looks at them. Not hungry. Not angry. Interested, the way you're interested in a bus that might be about to move.

Then it turns, walks down to the shoreline, and disappears along the water without a sound.

That's it. That's the whole encounter. No fight. No heroic stand. Just three people standing very still until the biggest animal they have ever seen decides they're not worth the trouble.

### The mistake

They wait. Ten heartbeats. Twenty.

Then Sena lets out a breath, and Tep starts crying, and Renn — Renn finally looks down at his hands.

He's been holding the spear backwards this entire time. The point has been behind him. Pointing at his own shoulder. He has been defending his family from a sabre-toothed cat with the blunt end of the only weapon he owns.

He turns it around. Quietly. Carefully. Like if he does it slowly enough, nobody will notice.

Sena notices.

Tep notices.

Tep stops crying, looks at Renn, and laughs so hard he has to sit down.

### The hook, coming back around

They walk home in the dark, all three of them, close together, and Renn does not talk for the entire way. Which, honestly, is the bravest thing he does all night.

So. Tomorrow night, when you're walking to your car, and you hear something behind you, and you speed up and tell yourself it was nothing —

You'll know exactly who you got that from.

A man by a lake. Two hundred thousand years ago. Same dark. Same sound. Same feeling in the back of the neck.

The only difference is what's out there now.

And the next time you hear the reeds move, you might want to check which end of the spear you're holding.

### Production notes for this version

- **Removed at your direction:** all spoken and on-screen disclaimers, all "this is imagined" lines, all "researchers think / it's debated" hedging, and the evidence-vs-fiction labels on the artwork.
- **The date is now stated plainly as 200,000 years**, which matches the 2025 dating study. Simpler for the story than a range, and defensible.
- **Names, dialogue, family relationships and the encounter are invented.** That's normal for this format and no longer announced in the video. Keep the private record in `video-03-factual-review.md` for your own reference — it is no longer something the audience hears.
- **The cat is now Homotherium**, which is the species actually found at the site, and the script describes it accurately: heavy shoulders, long forelimbs, protruding upper canines.
- **One deliberate departure from the old script:** the animal is now seen clearly. Since nothing is labelled any more, an unlabelled puma would have been the worst option; a described, correct *Homotherium* is both more accurate and more frightening.
