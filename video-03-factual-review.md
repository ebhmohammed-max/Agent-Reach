# Video 03 — factual review against sources

Reviewed for the *Human Before* Schöningen episode. Two findings are significant enough to require changes before publication; the rest are confirmations or precision fixes.

## 1. The date is contested — the script states ~300,000 years as settled

**Script lines affected:** line 3 (header note) and line 15 (narration: "roughly 300,000 years ago").

The script's figure comes from Leder et al. (2024), which describes Schöningen 13 II-4 as "this 300,000-y-old site (MIS 9)". That was the standard estimate. But in May 2025, Hutson et al. published amino acid geochronology run on snail opercula taken **directly from the find-bearing layer**, and revised the Spear Horizon to **~200,000 years** — roughly 100,000 years younger than previously claimed.

This is not a fringe result. It comes from LEIZA and Mainz with the dating method refined by Penkman's group at York, with supporting tests on horse teeth and ostracods from the same sediment. The earlier ~300,000 figure was always an inference from the layers above and below the horizon, never from the horizon itself.

**What this changes:**

- "About 300,000 years ago" should become "roughly 200,000 to 300,000 years ago", or the episode should state the revision.
- The revised age moves Schöningen into the Middle Palaeolithic and the time of **early Neanderthals**. That is a genuine opportunity: the episode currently never says who these people were. Naming them as probable Neanderthal relatives (with the appropriate caution) is both more accurate and more interesting than calling them "people".
- Re-recording is needed for line 15.

Sources: Hutson et al., *Science Advances* 11(19): eadv0752 (2025), https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adv0752 — press release: https://press.uni-mainz.de/age-of-schoningen-spears-revised-to-200000-years/

## 2. The episode omits the best predator evidence at the site

**Script line affected:** line 23 ("researchers have identified remains of large carnivores as well as evidence of human activity").

This is true but weaker than the record allows. The Spear Horizon produced five teeth and a humerus fragment from **two individual *Homotherium latidens***, the European sabre-toothed cat — and the humerus had been worked by hominins and used as a percussor. That makes Schöningen one of the best-documented *Homotherium* finds in an archaeological setting, and among the youngest in Europe.

So the honest situation is stronger and stranger than the script implies: the people at this lake were sharing it with a lion-sized sabre-toothed cat, and one of its bones ended up as somebody's tool. The script also currently jumps to a leopard at Swartkrans as its carnivore example, when the site it is actually about has a far more striking one.

**Caveat to preserve:** co-presence is not confrontation. The Tübingen authors suggest the spears may also have served for defence, but that is an interpretation, not a record of a fight. Keep the qualification exactly as the script already does elsewhere.

Sources: Serangeli et al., "The European saber-toothed cat (*Homotherium latidens*) found in the 'Spear Horizon' at Schöningen (Germany)", *Journal of Human Evolution* (2015); summary coverage: https://iflscience.com/plants-and-animals/our-ancestors-turned-saber-toothed-cat-bone-tool

## 3. The artwork is the wrong cat

The visuals show a mountain lion. Pumas are a New World animal and were never at Schöningen. The sabre-toothed cat is the animal actually evidenced there — but it is also not a creature to drop into a scene casually, because its build and proportions differ sharply from any modern big cat.

Two defensible options:

- **Show no cat at all.** Keep the encounter as a shape in the trees and reeds. This is the safest choice and costs nothing in accuracy.
- **Show *Homotherium*, clearly labelled.** Requires new artwork with the correct build — longer forelimbs, sloping back, distinctive dentition — and a label making clear it is a reconstruction.

Either way, the current mountain-lion frames should not ship.

## 4. Swartkrans — accurate, worth tightening

The script's wording is careful and correct. The skullcap is **SK 54**, a juvenile *Paranthropus robustus*, with puncture marks consistent with the lower canines of leopard specimen SK 349 from the same deposits (Brain, 1981). The script's phrase "a young hominin's skull" is defensible, but "a young *Paranthropus robustus*, a distant human relative" is more precise and reinforces the script's own point that this is not our species and not our place.

The existing qualification — that the marks show contact, not the moment itself — is right and should stay.

## 5. Wonderwerk — accurate, minor precision

The ~1 million year figure matches Berna et al. (2012), which found in-situ ash and burned bone in the Acheulean stratum. The script's note that earlier fire use has been proposed but is debated is also correct.

One addition worth making: the 2012 authors argued the burning was local and in situ rather than wind- or water-carried, which is precisely what makes it evidence of fire *at the site*. That is a stronger claim than "points to fire", and it is supported.

## Confirmed as stated

- Wooden spears preserved in lake deposits, found with butchered large animals — correct, and the 2024 paper confirms 187 wooden artefacts and at least 20 hunting weapons.
- Replica experiments show the spears could be thrown effectively, without proving how individual originals were used — correct.
- The open woodland setting (alder, birch, willow near water; pine on higher ground) matches the 2024 paper's reconstruction. The visuals are consistent with this.
- The framing that the encounter is invented, and that the bones establish a setting rather than a scene, is accurate and should stay in the narration.

2. Decide the cat: none, or an accurate *Homotherium*.
